Karnataka's Monsoon — The Big Picture
Dear Farmer, Karnataka receives the Southwest Monsoon from June to September, bringing 60–80% of the state's annual rainfall. Planning your kharif crops around this window is the single biggest decision of the farming year — get it right and your harvest doubles.

Karnataka is unique because it has four very different agro-climatic zones — from the heavy-rain Western Ghats coast to the dry Deccan Plateau. The same crop sown at the same time can thrive in Shivamogga and fail in Kalaburagi. This guide gives you district-specific advice so you plan for your land, not someone else's.

The monsoon arrives in Karnataka in two waves: the coast and Malnad get heavy rain from early June, while the plains of North Karnataka receive the bulk of their rain only from late June or early July. Timing your sowing to the first good soaking rain — not the calendar date — is the golden rule.

🌡️
Normal Onset: Southwest Monsoon reaches Karnataka's coast (Mangaluru) around June 1st, Bengaluru around June 10th, and North Karnataka (Kalaburagi, Vijayapura) by June 20–25th. Sow only after receiving at least 25mm of rain in 2–3 days.

Common Mistake: Farmers in the Southern Dry Zone often sow paddy expecting good rains — but this zone rarely gets enough water for paddy without irrigation. Ragi and groundnut are far more reliable choices for rainfed farming here.

First weeding for all crops (20–25 days after sowing). Top dress urea for ragi and maize. Scout for stem borer in paddy and maize. Apply neem-based spray as first line of defence.

🌸 Flowering Stage — Critical Care
Avoid water stress during flowering in ragi, maize, groundnut — a 7-day dry spell now reduces yield by 30–40%. If rain fails, provide one life-saving irrigation. Scout for blight and blast in paddy.

Best Crops by District

Match your district to the right kharif crop for the best yield with least risk:

District / Region Main Crops Rainfall Key Tip
Dakshina Kannada / Udupi Paddy, Coconut, Arecanut, Pepper 3000mm+ Drain excess water quickly; bund height matters.
Shivamogga / Hassan / Chikkamagaluru Paddy, Maize, Ragi, Coffee 1500–2000mm Sow maize by June 20 for best yield.
Bengaluru Rural / Tumakuru / Ramanagara Ragi, Groundnut, Sunflower, Mulberry 700–900mm Ragi is the safest choice — drought tolerant.
Mysuru / Mandya / Chamarajanagar Sugarcane, Paddy (irrigated), Ragi, Maize 750–900mm Sugarcane relies on Cauvery irrigation — plan water sharing.
Belagavi / Dharwad / Gadag Soybean, Maize, Cotton, Sunflower 600–800mm Soybean responds very well here — use improved varieties.
Kalaburagi / Vijayapura / Yadgir / Raichur Tur, Jowar, Cotton, Chickpea (rabi) 500–650mm Tur (pigeon pea) is most reliable — 160-day variety for this zone.
Bidar / Ballari / Koppal Tur, Sunflower, Cotton, Jowar 550–700mm Intercrop tur + jowar to hedge against dry spells.
Kodagu (Coorg) Coffee, Paddy, Cardamom, Black Pepper 2000–2500mm Coffee berry borer peaks in Aug–Sep — monitor closely.
✅
Pro Tip — Ragi for Dry Zones: Ragi (finger millet) is Karnataka's most drought-resilient crop. Varieties like GPU-28 and MR-6 yield 12–15 quintals/acre with just 500mm of well-distributed rain. Always the safest bet when forecast is uncertain.

Monsoon Farming Tips — Karnataka Specific
💧
Rainwater Harvesting
Build farm ponds before monsoon to capture runoff. Even a 10x10 metre pond at 2m depth stores enough for one life-saving irrigation over 1 acre.
🌱
Seed Treatment
Treat all seeds with Trichoderma (4g/kg) + Rhizobium (for legumes). This alone prevents 60% of early seedling loss from soil-borne diseases.
🏔️
Contour Bunding
In hilly areas (Malnad, Kodagu), contour bunds reduce soil erosion by up to 70% during heavy July–August rains. Repair before June 1st every year.
📱
Check Forecast Daily
Karnataka IMD issues district-level forecasts. Use KrashiMitra's weather tab — never spray pesticides 24hr before predicted heavy rain.
🐄
Green Manure Crops
Sow Dhaincha or Sunhemp in June as green manure. Incorporate into soil before flowering (45–50 days). Adds 60–80 kg N/acre naturally.
🌿
Intercropping
Intercrop ragi + tur or maize + soybean (4:1 rows). Reduces risk, adds nitrogen, and often earns more than mono-cropping in dry zones.

🚫
Avoid This: Do not sow paddy in the Southern Dry Zone (Tumakuru, Bengaluru Rural, Kolar) without assured irrigation. A failed paddy crop in a drought year can wipe out the entire season's investment. Ragi or groundnut carry far less risk.
✅ Key Takeaways — Remember These
  • Sow only after 25mm+ rain in 2–3 consecutive days — not by calendar date
  • Monsoon reaches coast (June 1) → Bengaluru (June 10) → North Karnataka (June 20–25)
  • Ragi (GPU-28 / MR-6) is the safest rainfed crop for dry zones — 500mm is enough
  • Coastal / Malnad farmers: paddy nursery ready by June 1, transplant by July 10
  • North Karnataka: tur + jowar intercropping hedges against erratic rain
  • Build farm pond and repair bunds before monsoon — not after first rain
  • September flowering stage is critical — one dry week costs 30–40% yield
